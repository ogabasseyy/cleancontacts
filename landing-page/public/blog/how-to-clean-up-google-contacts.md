

# How to Clean Up Google Contacts: The Complete Guide

Google Contacts is the default address book for over 2 billion Android users and anyone with a Gmail account. Over time, it collects contacts from emails, calendar invitations, Google Meet calls, and manual entries — creating a cluttered mess of duplicates, outdated entries, and contacts you don't recognize.

This guide covers every method to clean up your Google Contacts in 2026, from built-in tools to advanced techniques.

## Why Google Contacts Gets Messy

Google Contacts accumulates clutter from multiple sources:

- **Auto-created contacts** — Gmail creates "Other contacts" entries for anyone you've emailed, even once
- **Google Meet contacts** — Every meeting participant can generate a contact entry
- **Multiple Google accounts** — Personal and work accounts syncing to the same phone
- **App imports** — Third-party apps that sync with Google Contacts
- **SIM card transfers** — Importing contacts from an old SIM duplicates existing entries
- **Shared contacts** — Google Workspace shared directories mixing with personal contacts

The result? Most people have 2-3x more Google Contacts than actual people they know.

## Step 1: Access Google Contacts

You can clean up Google Contacts from three places:

- **Web** — Go to [contacts.google.com](https://contacts.google.com) (best for bulk cleanup)
- **Android** — Open the Google Contacts app
- **iPhone** — Go to contacts.google.com in Safari (Google Contacts app isn't available on iOS)

The web interface at contacts.google.com gives you the most control, so we recommend starting there.

## Step 2: Use Google's Built-in "Merge & Fix"

Google Contacts includes a "Merge & fix" feature that detects basic duplicates:

### On Desktop (contacts.google.com)

1. Go to [contacts.google.com](https://contacts.google.com)
2. Click **Merge & fix** in the left sidebar (or under "Fix & manage" on mobile)
3. Google shows duplicate suggestions with a merge preview
4. Click **Merge** for each pair, or **Merge all** to accept all suggestions at once

### On Android

1. Open the **Google Contacts** app
2. Tap **Fix & manage** in the bottom navigation bar
3. Tap **Merge & fix**
4. Review suggestions and tap **Merge**

### What Google's Tool Catches

- Exact name matches with different phone numbers or emails
- Contacts with the same phone number but different names
- Basic suggestions for adding missing information (phone numbers, emails)

### What Google's Tool Misses

- **Near-duplicates** — "Jon Smith" and "John Smith" won't be flagged
- **Cross-account duplicates** — The same person saved in your personal and work Google accounts
- **Contacts stored on your phone's device storage** — Google only sees Google account contacts
- **iCloud contacts on Android** — If you also sync iCloud, Google ignores those
- **Junk contacts** — Entries with no name, single characters, emoji-only names, or invalid data
- **Sensitive data** — Contacts with SSN or credit card numbers accidentally saved in notes

## Step 3: Clean Up "Other Contacts"

Google Contacts has a hidden category called **"Other contacts"** — these are people you've interacted with via Gmail, Calendar, or Google Chat but never explicitly saved. This list can contain hundreds or thousands of entries.

### How to Find Other Contacts

1. Go to [contacts.google.com](https://contacts.google.com)
2. Click **Other contacts** in the left sidebar
3. Review the list — you'll likely recognize very few names

### How to Delete Them

1. Click the checkbox at the top to select all
2. Click the **three-dot menu** (⋮)
3. Click **Delete** to remove them

Don't worry — these aren't "real" contacts. They're auto-generated suggestions. Deleting them won't affect your email history or conversations.

## Step 4: Use Labels to Organize

Google Contacts labels work like folders for your contacts:

### Create Labels

1. Go to [contacts.google.com](https://contacts.google.com)
2. Click **+ Create label** in the left sidebar
3. Name it (e.g., "Family", "Work", "Vendors")

### Assign Contacts to Labels

1. Select contacts using checkboxes
2. Click the **Label** icon (tag symbol)
3. Choose the label to apply

### Why Labels Help Cleanup

Once you organize contacts into labels, unlabeled contacts become obvious candidates for review or deletion. If a contact doesn't belong to any label after you've organized everything, it's probably junk.

## Step 5: Delete Contacts Without Phone Numbers

Many Google Contacts entries only have an email address — leftovers from Gmail's auto-create feature. These rarely belong in your phone's address book.

### How to Find Email-Only Contacts

1. In contacts.google.com, browse your contacts
2. Look for entries that show only an email address with no phone number
3. Select them and delete

This is tedious to do manually for large contact lists. **Contacts Cleaner** can automatically detect and flag contacts without phone numbers across all your accounts.

## Step 6: Fix Phone Number Formatting

Phone numbers saved in different formats cause multiple problems:

- "555-1234" vs "+15551234" may not match for caller ID
- WhatsApp can't identify contacts without international format
- Duplicate detection fails when the same number has different formatting

### Google's Formatting

Google Contacts automatically formats some numbers, but it's inconsistent — especially with international numbers or numbers imported from other sources.

## Step 8: Prevent Future Clutter

Once your Google Contacts are clean, prevent them from getting messy again:

### Turn Off Auto-Create

1. Go to [contacts.google.com](https://contacts.google.com)
2. Click the **gear icon** (Settings)
3. Under "Interaction information", uncheck **"Automatically add contacts of people you interact with"**

This stops Google from creating "Other contacts" entries for every email you send.

### Pick One Primary Account

If you have multiple Google accounts, choose one as your primary contact store. Move contacts from secondary accounts and stop syncing contacts on the others.

### Disable Account Sync for Contacts (on Secondary Accounts)

On Android:
1. Go to Settings > Accounts
2. Tap each non-primary Google account
3. Turn off **Contacts** sync

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will cleaning up Google Contacts affect my Gmail?

No. Deleting a contact doesn't delete your email conversations with that person. Gmail keeps all emails regardless of your contact list.

### Can I recover deleted Google Contacts?

Yes. Google Contacts has a **Trash** folder that keeps deleted contacts for 30 days. You can also restore contacts to any point in the last 30 days using the "Undo changes" feature.

### Do changes sync across all my devices?

Yes. Google Contacts syncs across all devices signed into the same Google account. Clean up on one device and the changes appear everywhere.

### What about contacts stored on my phone's device storage?

Google's tools only manage Google account contacts. Device-stored contacts need to be managed separately — or use **Contacts Cleaner**, which sees and cleans all contact sources.
